Problem 10

Reflection

What the evidence showed

Ticket 101 earned an A-. Boss sent the request to Inventory first, which was the smallest sensible delegation. The MCP result was specific: size S had zero units in Aisle B. The final decision—ask Tauhid whether to change size or wait—was justified because the system had no replenishment date. A general-purpose agent could have answered this ticket, but the specialist boundary made the evidence and the no-promise rule explicit.

Ticket 102 earned an A for control quality, with the workflow intentionally stopping short of payment. Accounting verified lease 1, the $2,400 amount, the September 2 due date, no prior payment, and $3,400 in checking. Boss prepared request #1, but the audit trail and database both show that it stayed pending. That is an important success: a recommendation did not become a cash movement. The remaining human approval is a limitation of the run, not evidence that approval happened.

Ticket 103 earned a B+. Boss used Accounting for price and obligation context, then Inventory for the 20-unit fulfillment question. The tools showed an $22 unit cost, $58 list price, no payable obligation, and only 8 medium hoodies—12 fewer than requested. The pending human review outcome was reasonable, although the sequence could be more efficient if Inventory checked quantity before Accounting examined financial context.

Across all three runs, MCP results matched independent database checks and the audit trail made the delegation, tool calls, reports, decisions, and pending request visible. The main cost of specialization was coordination and extra model/tool calls. A single agent would be cheaper and probably adequate for Ticket 101, but separating financial authority from inventory evidence made Ticket 102 safer and isolated the stock/pricing facts in Ticket 103. The team still cannot safely promise restocks, source external inventory, authorize discounts, or pay a vendor without additional data, integrations, and human authority.

The biggest surprise was that an invoice reference on Ticket 101 did not justify paying it: the customer request was blocked by stock, and the invoice could be a supplier obligation rather than a customer payment. If I continued, I would add a clearer specialist handoff protocol, reduce redundant ticket inspections, stream audit events to the dashboard during long runs, and add explicit approval/rejection history. The current evidence is 97 audit events, zero executed payments, checking unchanged at $3,400, and one pending $2,400 request. No post-approval cash result is claimed because no approval was authorized.
